A year on from tariffs meant to reshore US automotive manufacturing, the data points the other way: supply chains are reorienting toward the lowest-tariff suppliers rather than coming home.

- Only 2% of automotive leaders surveyed report completed reshoring efforts, and 38% say they aren't even considering it.
- US automotive import penetration fell from 44% to 39.6% between 2024 and 2025 — but that reflects a shrinking overall market, not expanded domestic production. Real imports fell 10% and real domestic output fell 1.3% over the same period.
- Every major import source contracted: Mexico (-6%), Canada (-8%), Japan (-11%), South Korea (-16%), Germany (-20%), and China (-25%).
- North American suppliers gained relative share due to lower tariff exposure — a reorientation toward the lowest-tariff suppliers, not a manufacturing revival.
- Policy uncertainty and the multi-year timelines required for capital investment help explain why reshoring decisions remain slow.
